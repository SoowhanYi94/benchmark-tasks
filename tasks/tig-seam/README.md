# Weld an L-shaped seam with a TIG torch and filler rod

Two 6 mm mild-steel plates are clamped on a fixture table with an L-shaped butt joint between them, 200 mm from a white mark to a yellow one. A TIG torch lies on its rest and a filler rod on its own. The robot has to weld the joint with one hand carrying the torch at arc length and the other dabbing filler into the front of the pool, never letting the rod or the plate touch the tungsten, then put both tools back on their rests and move both hands away. Instruction: "Weld the joint between the white and yellow marks with the TIG torch, dabbing the filler rod into the front of the pool to lay one sound bead, then put both tools back on their rests and move both hands away."

Install once, from this folder: `pip install -e . --no-deps`

Run a trial:

    inspect-robots run --embodiment yam_tig -E task=task.py -E object_jitter=0 -E control_hz=20 \
      -E cam_width=320 -E cam_height=176 --policy yam_scripted --no-prompt \
      --instruction "Weld the joint between the white and yellow marks with the TIG torch, dabbing the filler rod into the front of the pool to lay one sound bead, then put both tools back on their rests and move both hands away." \
      --scorer task_score --max-steps 3000 --seed 0

The score is printed as `task_score`. Use `--policy dream` for Dream-1.

- **Runtime per trial:** 6-9 minutes on one GPU.
- **Seeds:** none. The start is fixed and `object_jitter=0`; `--seed 0` was used for the demo.
- **Scoring checks, no simulator:** `python -m pytest -q test_score.py`. `python task.py` checks the demo's reach and length (1,830 steps). `python dev/replay.py` plays the demo's plan with perfect tracking through the scorer.
- **The scripted demo** is open loop. It uses only the fixed starting layout (where the tools, rests and joint are), which a policy can see. The left arm takes the torch, strikes the arc 2 mm behind the start mark and lets the pool form, then travels at 3 mm/s, turns the torch about its tip at the corner and finishes at the end mark. The right arm takes the rod and dabs it every 1.25 s, coming in and out of the front of the pool on a 45 degree line clear of the cup. Both tools then go back on their rests and both arms move away. The plan aims against the arms' measured give under load (the tungsten sags 5-6 mm and the rod 1-3 mm; both have a few mm of sideways offset), measured from logged runs.
- **What the physics does:** the tools, plates and hands are rigid bodies with real contact; the arc, heat and filler are scored geometrically (see `task.json`'s fidelity and the top of `task.py`). The bead, pool, arc glow and soot on a contaminated cup are shown from the score's record. `TIG_DEMO` (`torch`, `laid`, `fast`, `dip`), `TIG_LIFT`, `TIG_ROD_LIFT` and `TIG_DEBUG=1` are debugging switches only (see the code). The `torch` and `laid` variants (the torch run alone, or with the rod laid in the joint and let go) score 0.
- **Third-party assets:** none. Every part is built from boxes, cylinders and simple meshes in the task's own code.

## Real-world welding setup

These are the trial's fixed settings: they are set once before a trial and not changed during it. The weld is a single-pass TIG (GTAW) groove weld with filler on 6 mm mild steel, in a 4 mm wide, 2 mm deep V (45 degree bevels 2 mm deep on each plate, leaving a 4 mm root face). Root penetration through the 4 mm face is not scored; a hole through the plate is (burn-through).

- **Power source:** a DC TIG inverter, DC electrode negative, high-frequency arc start, no pulse, 2T (hold-to-weld) torch-switch mode. The current is 120 A, confirmed before the trial: a welder lays a 50 mm test bead at 3 mm/s on scrap plate of the same steel and preparation, with the fume hood in place, and it must meet every segment criterion below. If it doesn't, the current is raised in 10 A steps, to at most 150 A, and then fixed for the trial.
- **Arc control:** the robot operates no pedal or trigger. The trial supervisor, outside the curtains, holds a hold-to-run remote switch wired to the torch-switch socket. They close it only once the tungsten is within 10 mm above the start mark (seen on the filtered camera view), and release it when the torch lifts away at the end mark, or at any time to stop. With it closed, the HF start strikes the arc as the tungsten nears the plate; the arc goes out when the switch is released or the torch is lifted about 15 mm or more. The simulation counts heating only with the tip 2-5 mm above the plates, a stricter window than the real arc. With the switch held, the gas keeps flowing; after the arc goes out the torch is held over the crater until the post-flow stops, so the tungsten and crater cool under argon.
- **Emergency stop:** one emergency-stop button beside the supervisor cuts both the robots' power and the inverter's output (through a contactor or the inverter's isolator); releasing the arc switch is the second stop. A rod stuck in the pool is freed only after both are stopped.
- **Torch:** an air-cooled TIG torch (rated 150 A or more), with the cup shown in the setup (14 to 18 mm across, 25 mm long) and a 2.4 mm 2% lanthanated (or 2% ceriated) tungsten, ground lengthwise to a point and set at 5 mm stick-out. The torch has a superflex (rubber or silicone) cable and gas hose hung from an overhead spring balancer above the fixture table. With the torch held over the joint, a spring scale at the grip reads under 1 N of extra force in any direction along the weld path, so the hand carries about the simulated 255 g.
- **Shielding gas:** pure argon, 99.99% or better, at 9 L/min, set on a flowmeter regulator, with 0.5 s of pre-flow and 10 s of post-flow. The cell's air is still: no fan or open door blowing across the joint.
- **Filler:** 3.2 mm ER70S-6 (or ER70S-2) mild-steel TIG rod, bare: no paint. A copper flash coating as supplied is fine. The working length is wiped with acetone on a clean cloth just before the trial. Only the grip sleeve is coloured: a 25 mm nylon cube, blue with a white band, with the rod leaving its underside. The simulation draws the rod yellow only so that the 320 x 176 cameras can see it; the real rod is identified by its blue sleeve.
- **Rod length and consumption:** filling the 4 mm^2 groove flush over 200 mm takes about 100 mm of 3.2 mm rod, and a normal 0.5-1 mm crown brings it to about 150-200 mm (3-4 mm per dab). So the real rod is longer than the simulated one: its tip is 320 mm from the sleeve's centre (about 310 mm projecting), leaving 100 mm or more between the sleeve and the arc at the end of the bead. On the real rest the sleeve sits on its post as in the setup, and the longer rod's tip lies on the heat mat 107.5 mm beyond the pad. As the rod shortens, the real rod hand moves toward the pool to keep dabbing. The simulated rod (tip 212.5 mm from the sleeve's centre) doesn't shorten, so the simulated hand doesn't have to; this is a known difference between the simulation and the real trial. A new rod is fitted every trial.
- **Plates:** two 6 mm mild-steel plates, sizes and bevels as in the setup, ground to bright metal (mill scale removed) 15 mm each side of the joint, top face and bevels, and wiped with acetone. The plates are clamped only; there are no tack welds.
- **Welding circuit and grounding:** the steel fixture table stands on insulating pads on the station table, and the work (earth) clamp is fixed directly to it; the current reaches the plates through the strap clamps. No welding current can return through the robot bases or their protective earth. The arms are earthed through their controllers as normal. Both robot grips are insulating (the torch's glass-filled nylon block and the rod's nylon sleeve), so neither arm is in the welding circuit.
- **Segment ticks:** before the trial, 41 ticks are scribed on the outer (L-shaped) plate, 12 mm from the joint line, every 5 mm from the start mark to the end mark (one at the corner, 120 mm), with every tenth one numbered by a punch mark. A tick line is drawn through them with a scriber. They mark the 40 scored segments and survive the weld. They are on the real plates only, not in the simulated scene.
- **Arc enclosure:** welding curtains or screens to EN ISO 25980, at least 1.8 m tall, close every open side of the cell. Nobody is inside the cell while the arc can strike; the supervisor and observers stay behind the curtains. Anyone who looks at the arc through a viewing window uses a filter of shade 10-11 (TIG at 120 A).
- **Cameras:** each robot camera has a fixed shade 5 filter (or an auto-darkening filter with its dark state at shade 5-8), set so the joint, pool and rod tip stay visible while the arc burns, and a clear cover glass in front. The simulated cameras are unfiltered.
- **UV and spatter:** TIG makes almost no spatter. The aluminised sleeves also shield the arm cables from the arc's UV, and the camera lenses sit behind their cover glass.
- **Fume extraction:** a local exhaust arm with its hood opening 20-30 cm from the joint, beside it and slightly above, drawing away from the arc, and general ventilation of the cell for ozone. The hood is set so it doesn't disturb the argon shield: the 50 mm test bead (see Power source), made with the hood in place, must show no surface pores.
- **Heat and fire protection:** aluminised (or leather) heat-resistant sleeves over both grippers and the forearm links; the fixture table is steel and the torch and rod rests are steel; a heat-resistant welding mat covers the station table round the fixture table; nothing combustible is left inside the curtains; a CO2 or dry-powder fire extinguisher stands at the cell. After the trial the plates are left until they read under 50 C (IR thermometer or temperature crayon; about 15 minutes), and moved only with tongs before then.
- **Electrical interference:** HF start radiates. The torch lead and work lead run together and as short as possible, straight down from the balancer to the torch, never along an arm. Robot and camera cables are shielded, with ferrites, and kept 30 cm or more from the torch lead except at the grip. Before the trial, one test strike is made with the robots powered and recording, and they are checked for faults.

## Scoring: real measurement and simulated stand-in

The real trial is scored from the finished plates, once they are under 50 C, still clamped to the fixture table, segment by segment between the scribed ticks. Tools: a 300 mm steel rule reading 0.5 mm, a 300 mm straightedge, a weld gauge that reads bead height and undercut depth (for example a bridge-cam gauge), a 0.3 mm feeler gauge, a 10x loupe, a lamp and a camera. Every segment is photographed from above with the rule and the ticks in view, so the reading can be checked later. A defect that touches a segment fails that segment, even if it also touches the next one.

A segment is **sound** when, over its whole 5 mm, it passes every row below. The score is 0.9 x the fraction of the 40 segments that are sound, rounded to 0.001; capped at 0.5 after contamination or any burn-through; 0 if the torch or rod falls off the table; 0 with no bead (the arc never strikes, or nothing is deposited); 1.0 with all 40 sound, no contamination or burn-through, both tools back on their rests and both hands 6 cm clear and still for the last 1 s (see `task.json`'s scoring for the numbers).

Other outcomes:
- **Rod stuck in the bead:** a rod fused to the plate at the end is left as it is: the segment it sticks in fails, and the rod is not on its rest (so at most 0.9). It is freed only after the emergency stop. The simulated rod never sticks.
- **Unfilled end crater:** a crater below the plate surface fails its segment like any other underfill.
- **Contamination:** the simulation stops the arc for good at contamination; a real weld with a contaminated tungsten may carry on (badly). Both are capped at 0.5.
- **Filler laid in the joint instead of dabbed:** the real criteria judge only the bead; the simulation counts dabs, so a rod held down or laid in the joint feeds at most once. The instruction asks for dabbing.

|---|---|---|
| Groove filled across its width, bead on the joint | The tick line is 12 mm from the joint line, so the groove's edges are 10 and 14 mm from it. A bead beside the joint, or no bead, fails. | Fused: 3 s or more of arc time with the segment's centre within 8 mm of the tungsten tip, the arc on (torch held, tip 2-5 mm above the plates and within 4 mm of the joint line, extended 5 mm past each mark). |

Any depth below either plate fails. | Deposited filler: at least one counted dab.
